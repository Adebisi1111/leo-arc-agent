## A Biographical Study of Sheikh Abdul-Wahab Banni (Afonta) and His Contributions to Islamic Education and Scholarship in Osogbo (1978–2023)

### Background to the Study

The spread of Islam in Yorubaland has been closely associated with the activities of Islamic scholars who established Qur'anic schools, Arabic institutions, mosques, and centres of learning (Gbadamosi, 1978; Peel, 2000; Reichmuth, 1998). Through teaching, preaching, and scholarship, these scholars played significant roles in the religious, educational, and social development of their communities. Over time, Islamic education became one of the major channels through which religious knowledge, moral values, and intellectual traditions were transmitted from one generation to another.

Osogbo occupies an important position in the history of Islam in Southwestern Nigeria. Although the town is widely known as a centre of Yoruba cultural heritage and the annual Osun-Osogbo Festival, it has also developed into a notable centre of Islamic scholarship (Folorunsho, 1996; Rauf & Fahm, 2022; Olugunna, 1987). The growth of Islam in the town was sustained by scholars who established Qur'anic schools, Arabic institutions, and mosques (Folorunsho, 1996; Rauf & Fahm, 2022), thereby contributing to the expansion of Islamic education and the training of successive generations of Muslim scholars. Consequently, Osogbo has remained a town where Islamic and traditional religious practices have coexisted over the years.

Among the scholars who contributed significantly to the growth of Islamic education in Osogbo was Sheikh Abdul-Wahab Banni (Afonta). Born in Ilorin, Kwara State, in 1953, he received his early Islamic education within the renowned Ilorin tradition of Arabic and Islamic scholarship (Reichmuth, 1998; Jamiu, 2014) before relocating to Osogbo (National Insight, 2023; OsunDefender, 2023a, 2023b). In 1978, he established Mahad Zumratu Soliheena, an institution dedicated to the teaching of Arabic and Islamic studies (Banni, 2024). Through this institution, his public preaching, Qur'anic exegesis (Tafsir), writings, and leadership at Afonta Central Mosque, he devoted over four decades to the promotion of Islamic education and scholarship in Osogbo until his death in 2023 (National Insight, 2023; OsunDefender, 2023a, 2023b; Banni, 2024).

Despite his prominence within the Muslim community and his long-standing contributions to Islamic education, Sheikh Abdul-Wahab Banni has received little attention in academic historical studies. Most discussions on the history of Islam in Southwestern Nigeria have concentrated on the broader spread of Islam (Gbadamosi, 1978; Peel, 2000) or on prominent Islamic centres such as Ilorin (Reichmuth, 1998; Jamiu, 2014), while the contributions of individual scholars at the local level have received comparatively less attention. Where individual Osogbo scholars have been profiled in dedicated studies, as in Olugunna's (1990) biography of Alhaji Ashiru Uthman, Afonta remains absent from the record. Consequently, there is limited historical documentation on Sheikh Abdul-Wahab Banni's life, educational activities, institutional development, and scholarly contributions (Folorunsho, 1996; Rauf & Fahm, 2022).

It is against this background that this study examines the life of Sheikh Abdul-Wahab Banni (Afonta) and evaluates his contributions to Islamic education and scholarship in Osogbo between 1978 and 2023. By doing so, the study seeks to contribute to the historiography of Islam in Osun State (Makinde, 2007) and provide a historical account of the role of local Islamic scholars in the educational and religious development of their communities.

### Statement of the Problem

The history of Islam and Islamic education in Southwestern Nigeria has received considerable scholarly attention. Existing studies have examined the spread of Islam among the Yoruba (Gbadamosi, 1978), the establishment of Islamic institutions, and the contributions of Muslim scholars in major centres such as Ilorin, Ibadan, and Lagos (Peel, 2000; Reichmuth, 1998; Jamiu, 2014). Similarly, studies on Osogbo have explored the growth of Islam, Arabic literary activities (Folorunsho, 1996; Rauf & Fahm, 2022; Olugunna, 1987), and the interaction between Islam and the town's socio-cultural environment. However, despite these contributions, little scholarly attention has been devoted to the life and contributions of Sheikh Abdul-Wahab Banni (Afonta), one of the notable Islamic scholars who played a significant role in the development of Islamic education in Osogbo. The available academic literature on Islam in Osogbo (Folorunsho, 1996; Rauf & Fahm, 2022) does not treat him as a subject of study.

Sheikh Abdul-Wahab Banni established Mahad Zumratu Soliheena in 1978 (Banni, 2024) and spent over four decades promoting Islamic education through teaching, preaching, Qur'anic exegesis (Tafsir), mosque leadership, and the training of students (National Insight, 2023; OsunDefender, 2023a, 2023b). Despite these contributions, there is no comprehensive historical study that critically examines his life, educational activities, methods of scholarship, institutional development, and overall contribution to the growth of Islam in Osogbo.

Furthermore, most of the available information on Sheikh Abdul-Wahab Banni is contained in commemorative publications, sermons, newspaper reports, and accounts produced by his family members, students, and followers (OsunDefender, 2023a, 2023b; National Insight, 2023; Voice Air Media, 2023; Banni, 2024). While these materials provide valuable information, they are largely descriptive and devotional in nature and do not present a critical historical analysis of his life and contributions (cf. Folorunsho, 1996; Rauf & Fahm, 2022, for the scholarly treatment such materials lack). As a result, an important aspect of the historiography of Islam and Islamic education in Osogbo remains insufficiently documented. This study therefore adopts oral-history and archival methods suited to recovering under-documented religious biographies (Na'Allah, 2020).

This study therefore seeks to fill this gap by undertaking a historical examination of the life of Sheikh Abdul-Wahab Banni (Afonta) and assessing his contributions to Islamic education and scholarship in Osogbo from 1978 to 2023. In doing so, the study will contribute to the historiography of Islam in Osun State (Makinde, 2007) and deepen our understanding of the role of local Islamic scholars in the religious and educational development of Southwestern Nigeria.

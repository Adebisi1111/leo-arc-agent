## PART B — JUSTIFICATION (anchored on the Rauf & Fahm 2022 article you sent)

### B1. What the article you sent actually does
Rauf & Fahm (2022) is the **single most on-point existing study** for your topic: it examines Islam in Osogbo specifically, from a Muslim scholarly perspective, treating the town's religious life (Christianity, Islam, traditional religion) as interacting traditions. It is peer-reviewed (*Millah*, UII Yogyakarta), current (2022), and Osogbo-grounded.

### B2. Why it justifies your study (the gap it leaves)
The article studies Osogbo Islam at the level of **communities and traditions** — it does NOT profile any individual cleric, and Afonta is never named. That is exactly the opening your proposal fills:
> The best existing Osogbo scholarship (Rauf & Fahm, 2022) documents the town's religious field collectively; no study has yet profiled the individual local scholars who animated it. Afonta is the clearest case.

So the article is both your **closest precedent** and your **gap evidence** — cite it in the Background (Osogbo is a serious subject of study) AND in the Problem (it proves the field stops at the collective level).

Every other reference either (a) shows the field reaches Osogbo but stops at the collective (Folorunsho, Olugunna 1990), (b) explains where Afonta came from (Reichmuth, Jamiu), or (c) supplies the method and facts Fahm's article lacks for an individual life (Na'Allah, news, family book).

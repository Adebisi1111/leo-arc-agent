# Justification of Each Reference — Afonta Biographical Study

For every reference: WHY we use it, WHICH draft section it supports, and WHAT JOB it does.
Jobs: CONTEXT = background claim; GAP = proves the field ignores him; METHOD = justifies oral history; PRIMARY = biographical fact on Afonta himself.

---

## A. ACADEMIC SOURCES

**1. Falola & Akinyemi (2016), Encyclopedia of the Yoruba**
- Job: CONTEXT
- Lands in: Background §1
- Why: General authoritative frame on Yoruba society/religion. Supports the opening "Islam spread via scholars" claim without over-claiming. Safe, citable bedrock.

**2. Folorunsho (1996), Arabic literary activity among Osogbo Muslims**
- Job: CONTEXT + GAP (dual)
- Lands in: Background §2 & §4; Problem §2 & §5
- Why: THE anchor Osogbo source. Proves Osogbo had a real Arabic/Islamic scholarly culture he joined (context). But it treats Osogbo Muslims *collectively* — so we also use it as gap evidence: the field documents the *community*, not the *man*.

**3. Gbadamosi (1978), Growth of Islam among the Yoruba (1841–1908)**
- Job: CONTEXT (early period only)
- Lands in: Background §1; Problem §1
- Why: Classic survey of Islam's spread in Yorubaland. Supports the "Islam came via scholars/schools" line. Strictly background — ends 1908, so we NEVER cite it for Afonta's life (he was born 1953). Its silence on the modern era is itself gap evidence.

**4. Jamiu (2014), Ilorin scholars & Al-Iluri**
- Job: CONTEXT (his training tradition)
- Lands in: Background §3; Problem §1
- Why: Afonta trained in the Ilorin tradition. This paper documents that Ilorin/Al-Iluri lineage as a real, influential scholarly ecosystem — giving academic weight to "he received education within the renowned Ilorin tradition" without inventing a specific enrolment we can't prove.

**5. Makinde (2007), Sharīʿah in Oyo and Osun States**
- Job: SIGNIFICANCE (Osun frame)
- Lands in: Background §5; Problem §6
- Why: A doctoral study centred on Osun State's Islamic history. Used to position the study as contributing to the *Osun* historiography specifically — shows the regional field exists and this work plugs a gap in it.

**6. Na'Allah (2020), Yoruba Oral Tradition in Islamic Nigeria**
- Job: METHOD
- Lands in: Problem §5
- Why: Justifies the oral-history / archival approach as academically legitimate. Since Afonta's record is thin and devotional, we must defend *why* oral history is valid — this book is the scholarly backing. Pre-empts the "that's not real history" objection.

**7. Olugunna (1987), History of Osogbo**
- Job: CONTEXT (town background)
- Lands in: Background §2; Problem §2
- Why: Dedicated history of Osogbo. Supports "Osogbo is a historic town with coexisting religious traditions." Establishes the setting before we introduce Afonta.

**8. Olugunna (1990), Life & Arabic works of Alhaji Ashiru Uthman of Osogbo**
- Job: GAP (contrast proof — strongest)
- Lands in: Background §4; Problem §3
- Why: This is the key gap weapon. It's a full biography of a *different* Osogbo scholar. We cite it to show: "Osogbo scholars HAVE been individually profiled — just not Afonta." That converts a vague "little attention" claim into a concrete, demonstrable gap.

**9. Oyeweso (2012), Islam and Society in Osun State** ⚠️ title/publisher unverified
- Job: SIGNIFICANCE (Osun frame)
- Lands in: Background §5; Problem §6
- Why: Directly on Osun State Islamic society — the exact historiography the study claims to contribute to. Author + year confirmed real; confirm exact title/publisher from your copy before submitting.

**10. Peel (2000), Religious Encounter and the Making of the Yoruba**
- Job: CONTEXT (Yoruba religion)
- Lands in: Background §1; Problem §1
- Why: Landmark study of Yoruba religious history. Supports the broad "Islam is central to Yoruba religious development" claim and places Afonta's story in the larger field.

**11. Rauf & Fahm (2022), Religious Encounters in Osogbo: A Muslim Perspective**
- Job: CONTEXT + GAP (dual) — this is your original "Fahm (2022)"
- Lands in: Background §2 & §4; Problem §2 & §5
- Why: Recent (2022), Osogbo-specific Islam study. Great for current context. Like Folorunsho, it's collective — so it doubles as gap evidence that even modern Osogbo Islam scholarship stays at the community level, not the individual. Corrects the mis-attributed "Fahm (2022)" to Rauf & Fahm.

**12. Reichmuth (1998), Islamic education & social integration in Ilorin**
- Job: CONTEXT (Ilorin as learning centre)
- Lands in: Background §1, §3; Problem §1
- Why: The definitive work on Ilorin as a centre of Islamic learning. Backs both "Islam spread via scholars" (§1) and "Afonta trained in the Ilorin tradition" (§3). Shows the ecosystem that produced him.

## B. PRIMARY SOURCES ON AFONTA (biographical facts)

**13. Banni, M. Q. A. (2024), commemorative biography** [family book]
- Job: PRIMARY (facts) — flagged devotional
- Lands in: Background §3–§4; Problem §4–§5
- Why: The ONLY source for the 1978 founding date, his timeline, and family-level detail. We use it for the facts but explicitly flag it as devotional (your draft already does). It's why the study needs oral history to cross-check it.

**14. National Insight News (2023, Dec 17), "Banni Afonta dies at 70"**
- Job: PRIMARY (independent fact)
- Lands in: Background §3–§4; Problem §4
- Why: Independent confirmation of age 70 (→ born ~1953) and "chief imam of Afonta mosque." NOT family — this is the corroboration that makes the biography credible beyond the devotional book.

**15. OsunDefender (2023, Dec 16), "Afonta to be buried in Osogbo"**
- Job: PRIMARY (independent fact)
- Lands in: Background §3; Problem §4
- Why: Confirms "indigene of Ilorin who spent his lifetime in Osogbo" and that he founded Arabic schools/mosques. Independent press — strengthens the relocate-to-Osogbo and institution claims.

**16. OsunDefender (2023, Dec 17), "Corpse arrives Freedom Park"**
- Job: PRIMARY (independent fact — death)
- Lands in: Background §4; Problem §4
- Why: Confirms the janazah / death date and public standing. Corroborates the 2023 death claim with a second independent outlet.

**17. Voice Air Media (2023, Dec 16), "To be buried Sunday"**
- Job: PRIMARY (independent fact)
- Lands in: Problem §5
- Why: Third independent source on the death/burial; the "Asiwaju Adini of Afonta mosque" quote confirms his mosque leadership role. Used in the "available info is news + commemorative" sentence to show the material exists but is non-critical.

## C. WHY WE DROPPED TWO

- **Quadri (1999), Sufism in SW Nigeria** — not a findable real source. Including it risks a supervisor finding nothing = credibility hit. Dropped.
- **Balogun Solagberu (2018), Tijaniyyah in Ilorin** — real, but only relevant if the draft claims Afonta belonged to a Sufi order. The draft makes no such claim, so it was excluded to avoid an unsupported inference.
